Dreaming of Your Mother

mother, mom, mommy, mum · ●●●●● fairly positive, with warnings

“Because I feel that, in the Heavens above,
The angels, whispering to one another”

Edgar Allan Poe, “To My Mother”, 1849

Dreaming of your mother is one of the most common family dreams. It usually reflects your relationship with care, protection or independence, more than anything about her. The details matter: whether she is calm, ill, angry or gone tells you which of those themes the dream is working on.

Summary: Seeing your mother at peace promises success and good news; seeing her ill or dead brings sadness.

What happens in the dream

  • You see your mother at home and alive: joy and success in what you undertake
  • You talk with her: good news about matters you are anxious over
  • You quarrel with her: surprisingly, she will do something generous for you
  • She gives you a present: a birth in the family
  • You hear her calling you: you are neglecting your duties or on the wrong track
  • You hold her tenderly: a separation to come
  • You hear her crying in pain: her illness, or some trouble threatening you
  • You see her wasted or dead: sadness and misfortune

How you felt

  • Tenderness or longing: the dream often reflects your bond with her, past or present
  • A need for protection: it may show a wish to be reassured or guided

Ask yourself

  • What would I like to say to my mother?
  • What do I need in order to feel safe?

What the old dream books said

G. H. Miller1901

Seeing your mother at home promises success in what you undertake, and talking with her brings good news. Hearing her call you means you are neglecting your duties; hearing her cry in pain warns of illness or trouble.

F. Fontaine1862

Quarrelling with your mother is, surprisingly, a good sign: she will do something generous for you. Holding her tenderly foretells a separation, and receiving a present from her announces a new baby in the family.

A. H. Noe1885

Short and blunt: seeing your mother alive means joy, seeing her dead means misfortune.

Read these as tradition, not prediction.

What it often means today

Modern readers take the mother in a dream less as a person than as a role: the one who feeds, protects, judges or lets go. A warm, peaceful mother often appears in periods of fatigue or worry, when part of you wants to be looked after. A controlling or critical mother tends to show up when you are pushing for independence, at work or in a relationship, and still hear the old voice telling you what to do.

Jung made this the centre of growing up. In Psychology of the Unconscious (1912, translated 1916) he wrote that the child gradually becomes freed from his parents, and that “upon the degree in which this is accomplished depends his health and future well-being.” For Jung the mother also stands for anything that holds and shelters, which is why she can blur in dreams into a house, the sea or a city. Seen this way, a dream of your mother can be a dream about home and dependence in general.

A mother who has died is a different case. Such dreams are very common in grief and are often gentle: she is simply there, alive, sometimes saying very little. Today they are usually read as part of mourning rather than as messages. Becoming a mother yourself also brings her into dreams, as you compare the parent you are becoming with the one you had.

How common is it?

Family is one of the main casts of everyday dreams. In the standard Hall and Van de Castle norms, family members make up 15% of all the people in women’s dreams and 9% in men’s, and 38% of students in a large Canadian survey had dreamed of someone who has died as if they were alive.

Source: Hall & Van de Castle (1966), The Content Analysis of Dreams: normative tables from 500 men’s and 491 women’s dream reports (dreams.ucsc.edu/Norms). Nielsen et al. (2003), “The Typical Dreams of Canadian University Students”, Dreaming 13(4): 211–235. 1,181 first-year students were asked whether they had ever had each of 55 dream themes.

Frequently asked questions

Is dreaming of my dead mother a sign?

Old dream books read it as an omen, but nothing shows that it predicts anything. It is one of the most frequent dreams after a loss: in a survey of 278 bereaved hospice caregivers, 58% had dreamed of the person who died (Wright et al., 2014). Most people find these dreams comforting, and they tend to become less frequent as grief settles.

Why do I dream about my mother every night?

Repeated dreams usually follow what is on your mind during the day. A move, an illness in the family, a conflict with her or a new baby can all keep her in your dreams for weeks. If the dreams are distressing and do not fade, talking about the situation they point to often helps more than decoding them.

What does it mean to dream of fighting with your mother?

The old books found it surprisingly lucky: she would soon do something generous. Today it is usually read as a sign of tension between closeness and independence, often about a decision you want to make on your own. The dream lets you say what you might hold back when awake.
