Dreaming of a Mouse

mouse, rodent · ●●●●● mostly negative

“The best-laid schemes o’ mice an’ men
Gang aft agley”

Robert Burns, “To a Mouse”, 1785

Dreaming of a mouse is a small dream with a long history: for the old dream books it meant thieves, false friends and trouble at home. Today it is more often read as a sign of something small that keeps nagging at you, a worry you have not dealt with or a feeling of being overlooked.

Summary: A mouse points to sly enemies and petty theft, which you can still uncover.

What happens in the dream

  • You see a mouse or several: thieves in the house
  • You kill a mouse: you will catch a thief in the act
  • A mouse in general: an enemy who will annoy you with cunning and treachery
  • You pet a mouse: a dishonest sweetheart

How you felt

  • A quiet unease: the dream often reflects small worries nibbling at your peace
  • Feeling small: it may show you feel slight in the face of some situation

Ask yourself

  • Which small worry keeps coming back?
  • Who or what is taking something from me unnoticed?

What the old dream books said

G. H. Miller1901

Mice foretell domestic troubles and insincere friends, and business turning discouraging. Killing them means you will overcome your enemies; letting them escape means a doubtful struggle.

F. Fontaine1862

Seeing one mouse or several warns of thieves in the house; killing a mouse means you will catch the thief. For a young woman, petting a mouse points to a dishonest lover.

A. H. Noe1885

The opposite reading: mice mean prosperity, success in love and a happy marriage, and for a married woman, a child who will become rich and powerful.

Read these as tradition, not prediction.

What it often means today

A mouse is quiet, quick and hard to catch, and that is usually what the dream borrows. It often appears when something small but persistent is eating away at your peace: an unpaid bill, a remark you keep replaying, a household problem you keep putting off. Seeing mice run along the walls of your home ties the worry to family or private life.

How you react matters more than the animal. Calmly watching a mouse, or even finding it sweet, suggests you are at ease with something you once feared. Panic or disgust points to an anxiety that feels out of proportion. Catching or killing it is generally read as dealing with the problem at last, close to the old books’ idea of catching the thief.

A mouse can also stand for yourself. Feeling small, unnoticed or too timid to speak up, especially at work, can turn you into the mouse of the dream, hiding while larger creatures move around you.

How common is it?

Animals are a regular but modest part of adult dreams. In the Hall and Van de Castle norms, animals make up 6% of all the characters in men’s dreams and 4% in women’s; they are far more frequent in children’s dreams.

Source: Hall & Van de Castle (1966), The Content Analysis of Dreams: normative tables from 500 men’s and 491 women’s dream reports (dreams.ucsc.edu/Norms).

Frequently asked questions

Is dreaming of mice a bad omen?

The old books disagreed: Miller and Fontaine saw thieves and false friends, while Noe saw prosperity and a happy marriage. Nothing suggests these dreams predict anything. They are better read as a reflection of small worries.

What does it mean to dream of mice in my house?

A home stands for your private life. Mice in it usually point to small problems or tensions at home that you have not addressed yet.

What does it mean to dream of killing a mouse?

Traditionally it meant overcoming your enemies or catching a thief. Today it is often read as finally taking control of a nagging issue.
