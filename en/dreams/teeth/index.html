Dreaming of Teeth

teeth, dentures, fangs, molars · ●●●●● very negative, with a few happy exceptions

“Sans teeth, sans eyes, sans taste, sans everything.”

William Shakespeare, As You Like It, c. 1599

Dreaming of your teeth falling out is one of the classic dreams, and one of the most unsettling. The old dream books read it as a loss to come, often of a friend; today it is linked to stress, to fear of how others see you, and, according to one study, to something as concrete as a clenched jaw.

Summary: Teeth are among the darkest dreams in the tradition: illness, loss and bereavement, with a few bright exceptions.

What happens in the dream

  • You admire your white teeth: pleasant work and wishes fulfilled
  • A new tooth comes in: the birth of a child with a bright future
  • Decayed or broken teeth: strain on your health or business
  • A tooth is filled: you'll recover something you lost
  • You brush your teeth: you'll have to fight to keep your position
  • Tartar falls away leaving sound teeth: a passing ailment that leaves you wiser
  • Loose or falling teeth: setbacks, bad news, losing friends or loved ones
  • The more fall out, the worse it is: one means bad news; all of them, real hardship
  • A dentist pulls a tooth: a long illness

How you felt

  • Anxiety: this very common dream often mirrors a fear of losing control or looking bad
  • Vulnerability: worry about your health, your image or the people you love

Ask yourself

  • Where do I feel I'm losing my grip?
  • Am I worried about how I come across?

What the old dream books said

G. H. Miller1901

Loose teeth mean failures and gloomy news; losing them, burdens that will crush your pride; having them knocked out, sudden misfortune. Having them filled means recovering lost valuables.

F. Fontaine1862

Losing your teeth means losing friends, troubles and misfortune, and for a lover, losing their sweetheart’s affection. Cutting a new tooth announces the birth of a child who will make a name in the world.

A. H. Noe1885

Losing a tooth means losing a friend through death, with troubles to follow. Like Fontaine, Noe sees a new tooth as the birth of a child with a great future.

What it often means today

Teeth are visible, they matter for how we look and speak, and losing them is hard to reverse. That is why the most common modern reading links these dreams to fear of losing face, of ageing, or of losing control of a situation. They often turn up before an interview, a performance or any moment when you feel judged.

There is also a very physical explanation. Freud already mentioned that earlier writers blamed these dreams on “dental irritation”. In 2018, a study of 210 students by Rozen and Soffer-Dudek found that dreams of teeth falling out were linked to tension in the teeth, gums or jaw on waking, and not to psychological distress, while other dream themes were. Grinding or clenching your teeth at night may be enough to bring the dream on.

Both readings can be true at once: stress tends to make people clench their jaw, and the jaw then feeds the dream. If the dream keeps coming back and you wake with a sore jaw, it is worth mentioning to a dentist.

How common is it?

It is a typical dream, though less frequent than its reputation suggests: in a survey of 1,181 Canadian students, 19% had dreamed of their teeth falling out.

Source: Nielsen et al. (2003), “The Typical Dreams of Canadian University Students”, Dreaming 13(4): 211–235. 1,181 first-year students were asked whether they had ever had each of 55 dream themes. Rozen & Soffer-Dudek (2018), “Dreams of Teeth Falling Out: An Empirical Investigation of Physiological and Psychological Correlates”, Frontiers in Psychology 9: 1812.

Frequently asked questions

Does dreaming of teeth falling out mean someone will die?

That was the old reading, in Fontaine and Noe. Nothing supports it. The dream is better explained by stress or by tension in the jaw during sleep.

Why do I keep dreaming my teeth are falling out?

Repeated teeth dreams are often linked to grinding or clenching at night, and to periods of stress. A 2018 study found a link with jaw and gum tension on waking.

What does it mean to dream of a tooth breaking or crumbling?

It is usually read the same way as losing teeth: worry about your image, your health or a situation slipping out of your control.

What does it mean to dream of your teeth falling out?

Usually stress or fear of losing face, and sometimes simply a clenched jaw during sleep.

Is it normal to dream of your teeth falling out?

Yes. About one person in five has had this dream, and it is linked to grinding or clenching at night.

What does losing your teeth symbolize in a dream?

Image, confidence and control, and in the old books, losing a friend.
