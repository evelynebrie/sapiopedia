Vintage names making a comeback

25 names from our great-grandparents' generation that parents are choosing again, from Alfred to Mabel, with origin and meaning.

Names tend to come back after roughly a hundred years, once the people who wore them are grandparents or great-grandparents and the name sounds charming rather than dated. The 25 below were common in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries and are being given again across France, Britain and North America.

How to choose

  • Ask the family: a vintage name attached to a beloved grandparent carries a story, which is worth more than novelty.
  • Test the nickname. Alfred becomes Alfie or Fred, Marguerite becomes Margot or Daisy; make sure you like where it lands.
  • The comeback is uneven: a name that still sounds like a great-aunt in one country may already be everywhere in another, so listen to what you hear around you.

Meanings follow standard etymological dictionaries of given names; where scholars disagree, we say so. Popularity varies a great deal by country and by decade.
